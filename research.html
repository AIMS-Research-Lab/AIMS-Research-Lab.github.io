We build learning systems that reason like scientists and engineers: grounded in principles, honest about uncertainty, and useful for designing real things.

Overview

Simulation has transformed engineering, but it is slow, and data alone is rarely enough. Our work sits in between: models that combine the structure of physics with the flexibility of learning, and use both to explore designs no one has tried.

01 / 04

Learning with the laws of nature

Conventional networks learn only from examples. We train models on observations and on the equations that govern a system, so their predictions respect conservation, symmetry and boundary behaviour, even where measurements are sparse, noisy or missing entirely.

  • How can known physics regularise learning in data-poor regimes?
  • How do we train such models robustly on stiff, multi-scale problems?
  • Can we recover hidden parameters and laws from partial observations?
02 / 04

Learning entire solution spaces

Instead of solving one problem at a time, we learn mappings between functions. Our models take a geometry, a load or an initial state and return the full physical response, independent of resolution and many orders of magnitude faster than traditional solvers.

  • Architectures that generalise across shapes, meshes and boundary conditions
  • Reliable surrogates for real-time prediction and control
  • Quantifying when, and how much, a learned solver can be trusted
03 / 04

Architected matter

Some of the most surprising materials owe their behaviour to geometry, not chemistry. These structures widen when stretched, absorb impact, or reconfigure on demand. We model, understand and engineer these lattices, and use learning to navigate their huge design spaces.

  • Mechanics of lattice and cellular structures under large deformation
  • Data-driven discovery of geometries with target properties
  • Applications in protection, biomedical devices and lightweight structures
04 / 04

Intelligent design loops

Prediction is only half the story. We connect simulation, learning and fabrication in closed loops for inverse design, uncertainty-aware optimisation and data-efficient experimentation, so that computation leads directly to things worth building and testing.

  • Inverse design from desired behaviour back to structure
  • Combining high- and low-fidelity data efficiently
  • Open tools and benchmarks for scientific machine learning
